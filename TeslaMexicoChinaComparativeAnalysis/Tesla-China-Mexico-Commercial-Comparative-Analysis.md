## 1. Scope and Sources

This study compares Tesla’s sales operations in China and Mexico. It covers leadership, stores, charging, customer preferences, prices, sales, and competitors. Section 8 adds a short factory comparison focused on production, costs, and vehicle prices.

The study uses two research reports and their cited public sources. Tesla does not publish matching sales, store, and profit data for both countries. Mexican sales estimates are less certain because Tesla does not report its volumes to INEGI/AMIA. Estimated figures are labeled, and missing data are listed in Section 11.

## 2. Overview

Tesla entered both countries with imported premium cars, company-owned stores, online sales, and its own charging network. Neither country used a franchised dealer network.

In China, Elon Musk first promoted fair pricing and the Model S’s technology. Veronica Wu, who joined from Apple, expanded stores and set ambitious sales targets. Tesla registered **2,499 cars in 2014**, against a revised goal of 5,000. Customers faced problems with apartment charging, property-manager approvals, and Tesla’s incompatible charging plug. Tom Zhu became China general manager in December 2014 and focused on home charging, public chargers, and compatibility with China’s GB/T standard. Tesla sold approximately **11,000 cars in 2016**. China revenue reached **US$2.03 billion in 2017**, and estimated domestic sales reached **657,000 in 2024**.

Tesla entered Mexico through a Santa Fe store in December 2015. Its Model S cost around two million pesos in a market where many popular cars sold for about one-tenth of that amount. Tesla opened its first Mexican Superchargers in 2016, reached 21 stations by mid-2021 and approximately 35 by October 2024, and operated seven official stores by 2026. It cut the Model 3’s entry price by approximately **29% during 2024–2025**, offered financing and trade-ins, and opened part of its charging network to other brands in 2026. Estimated Tesla sales remained in the low thousands per year. Public data do not show whether these results met Tesla’s internal goals.

## 3. Entry Strategy

The two entries shared several features:

- Imported premium vehicles.
- Tesla-owned stores and online sales.
- Company-operated charging.
- Limited use of conventional advertising.
- Products and prices developed mainly for other markets.

China’s early strategy included rapid store expansion in major cities. Mexico added stores and chargers more gradually around large cities and highway corridors.

The public record also differs. China has named country leaders whose arrivals and departures match changes in strategy. The Mexico report does not name country leaders for most of 2015–2023. The Mexican account follows company actions during those years.

## 4. Market Entry

### China: Musk, Wu, and the 2014 Sales Miss

In January 2014, Tesla priced the Model S at **RMB 734,000**, approximately US$121,260 at the time. Musk said the price consisted of the U.S. price plus taxes, duties, and transport. Tesla avoided the extra luxury markup used by some imported-car brands, although taxes and other costs still placed the Chinese price roughly 65% above the U.S. sticker. Musk received extensive public attention when he delivered the first cars and predicted that China could supply up to 35% of Tesla’s global sales that year ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2014/01/23/tesla-announces-a-fair-price-for-china-model-s-%C2%A5-734000/); [MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/tesla-plots-china-turnaround-after-weak-sales-2015-06-22)).

**Veronica Wu** joined Tesla in December 2013 after working at Apple. She expanded flagship stores in major cities and pursued a target of **10,000–12,000 vehicles for 2014**. The target was later reduced to 5,000 ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2014-12-15/teslas-china-vp-steps-down-after-9-month-stint-101012833.html); [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2016/01/30/teslas-2016-china-sales-target-is-50-the-2015-target/)).

Many potential customers lived in apartment towers and used shared parking. Installing a home charger required approval from property managers, who raised concerns about electrical capacity and fire risk. Tesla’s plug was also incompatible with the GB/T standard used by China’s public charging network. These conditions limited the value of showroom demand when customers could not easily charge at home or in public ([East-West Center](https://www.eastwestcenter.org/sites/default/files/private/apb152harwit.pdf); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/09/tesla-china-cuts-jobs-after-slow-sales/)).

Customer expectations presented another issue. Chinese premium-car buyers often compared vehicles with long-wheelbase Audi A6L and Mercedes S-Class sedans. Many of these cars were chauffeur-driven, which increased the importance of rear-seat comfort. Some buyers also expected a service relationship associated with a dealership. Tesla emphasized acceleration, a minimalist interior, and a large touchscreen ([East-West Center](https://www.eastwestcenter.org/sites/default/files/private/apb152harwit.pdf)).

Tesla imported approximately **4,800 cars in 2014 and registered 2,499**. Kingston Chang left in March, and Wu left in December after about nine months. Musk acknowledged weak sales in early 2015. Tesla then cut approximately 30% of its Chinese staff, or about 180 of 600 employees ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2014-12-15/teslas-china-vp-steps-down-after-9-month-stint-101012833.html); [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/content/897614.shtml); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/09/tesla-china-cuts-jobs-after-slow-sales/)).

### Mexico: Premium Entry in 2015

Tesla entered Mexico in December 2015 through a direct-sales store in Santa Fe, Mexico City. The Model S started at around **two million pesos**, about ten times the price of some of the country’s best-selling vehicles. Charging access was a concern, and the purchase price placed Tesla far above most of the market ([El Cronista](https://www.elcronistayucatan.mx/2021/06/30/tesla-abre-tienda-en-merida/)).

Every Tesla sold in Mexico was imported. Exchange rates, transport, and double-digit borrowing costs affected prices and monthly payments. Federal purchase incentives were limited. Lower fuel and maintenance costs could reduce long-term ownership costs, but the upfront price and credit requirements remained ([Latamobility](https://latamobility.com/conoce-los-incentivos-fiscales-por-usar-vehiculos-electricos-en-mexico/); [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/es/industry-reports/mexico-electric-car-market)).

In **2016**, Tesla opened Latin America’s first Tesla Superchargers in Mexico, starting with the Cuernavaca corridor. Store expansion continued gradually over the following years ([Tesla Supercharger reference](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Supercharger); [El Cronista](https://www.elcronistayucatan.mx/2021/06/30/tesla-abre-tienda-en-merida/)).

## 5. Charging and Retail Expansion

### China: Tom Zhu and the Charging Network

**Tom Zhu** joined Tesla in April 2014 to build the charging network. His background was in project management and infrastructure. He studied commerce in New Zealand, earned an MBA at Duke, and co-founded an engineering project-management company with experience in Africa. When Wu left in December 2014, Zhu became general manager of Tesla China ([Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/student-network/alumni-ambassadors/tom-zhu); [The Wire China](https://automobility.io/2023/02/the-wire-china%EF%BC%9Atesla-tom/); [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/content/897614.shtml)).

Zhu’s work involved grid operators, property managers, and municipal governments. During his first year, Tesla built roughly **40 Supercharger stations and more than 600 destination-charging points** ([Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/content/897614.shtml)).

Tesla offered free home wall-connector installation and sent teams to negotiate with property managers for owners. The first Supercharger opened in Shanghai in April 2014. The network reached **56 stations by early 2015 and 114 by early 2017**, with additional destination chargers at hotels, shopping centers, and offices ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/09/tesla-china-cuts-jobs-after-slow-sales/); [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Tesla%20Supercharger%20Network/7737406)).

Tesla began shipping **GB/T adapters in 2017**, allowing customers to use China’s public network. Later vehicles for the Chinese market included native GB/T ports ([Pandaily](https://pandaily.com/tesla-sales-double-china-exceeding-2b-2017/)).

Sales and revenue increased during this period. Tesla sold approximately **11,000 vehicles in 2016**. China revenue rose from **US$318 million in 2015 to US$1.06 billion in 2016 and US$2.03 billion in 2017**. Charging, prices, product availability, government policy, and general EV-market growth all affected these results; the figures do not isolate the effect of charging ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2017/03/03/tesla-triple-sales-china-2016/); [Pandaily](https://pandaily.com/tesla-sales-double-china-exceeding-2b-2017/)).

### Mexico: Stores and Highway Charging

Mexico’s charging conditions included condominiums, street parking, long driving distances, uneven highway coverage, and a small public charging network. Tesla’s Supercharger network grew from the first Cuernavaca sites in 2016 to **21 stations by mid-2021** and approximately **35 by October 2024**. Sites were concentrated around Mexico City, Guadalajara, Monterrey, Puebla, Querétaro, and major highways ([El Cronista](https://www.elcronistayucatan.mx/2021/06/30/tesla-abre-tienda-en-merida/); [Latamobility](https://latamobility.com/tesla-cuenta-con-35-supercargadores-en-mexico/)).

At the end of 2025, Mexico’s Electro Movilidad Asociación counted **4,060 public charging positions** and more than **52,000 private or residential charging points**. Public positions increased 22% year over year, and fast charging increased 86.1%. Most charging points were private or residential ([EMA/BienInformado](https://bieninformado.mx/red-de-carga-para-vehiculos-electricos-en-mexico-crecio-26-en-2025/)).

Tesla expanded from the Santa Fe store to **seven official stores by 2026**: three in Mexico City, two in Guadalajara, one in Monterrey, and one in Mérida. Online sales and temporary pop-up stores added to this network ([El CEO](https://elceo.com/negocios/el-paso-de-elon-musk-en-mexico-tesla-se-frena-pero-starlink-avanza/); [El Cronista](https://www.elcronistayucatan.mx/2021/06/30/tesla-abre-tienda-en-merida/)).

China’s early charging expansion was faster in absolute numbers. The station counts have not been adjusted for population, geography, EV fleet size, stalls per station, or charger use. The China report also lacks a consistent annual store count. Sales per store, customer conversion rates, and city-level demand would be needed to assess whether either country had the right number of stores.

## 6. Products and Customer Preferences

### China: Local Software and Vehicle Choices

Tesla added Chinese services to its vehicles. Navigation used Baidu maps. Infotainment included QQ Music, NetEase karaoke, and local video applications. Voice recognition supported Mandarin, and WeChat formed part of the ownership and service experience. In 2025–2026, Tesla partnered with ByteDance’s Doubao and DeepSeek models for its Chinese voice assistant ([Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/tesla-advances-china-localisation-with-wechat-car-features/); [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/06/tesla-shanghai-plant-localization-98/)).

The Model Y launched in China in January 2021 at **RMB 339,900**, approximately RMB 150,000 below pre-sale expectations. It gave Tesla a family crossover alongside its sedans. Tesla introduced the six-seat Model Y L in 2025 as a China-first vehicle ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/tesla-starts-model-y-production-shanghai-china-reduces-price-2021-1)).

The China-made Model 3 launched at **RMB 355,800 in December 2019**, fell to RMB 291,800 in May 2020, and reached **RMB 249,900 in October 2020** after Tesla adopted CATL lithium-iron-phosphate batteries. The total reduction from launch was **29.8%**. Section 8 describes the related factory and supplier changes ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/05/01/tesla-vp-explains-why-china-made-model-3-drops-30000-yuan-in-price/); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/tesla-drops-model-3-price-china-cheaper-cobalt-batteries-lithium-2020-10)).

In April 2021, a customer protesting alleged brake failure climbed onto a Tesla display car at the Shanghai Auto Show. Tesla China executive Tao Lin’s initial response drew further criticism, and Tesla later apologized. The incident led to scrutiny of Tesla’s customer relations, quality complaints, and vehicle data ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2021/4/20/22394638/tesla-shanghai-auto-show-protest-brake-failure); [Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/news/tesla-brake-failure-protest-at-shanghai-auto-show-leads-to-online-uproar/)).

### Mexico: Brand Influence and Local Talent

Mexican coverage described a **“Tesla effect”** in public discussion of electric vehicles. Tesla’s presence helped draw attention to EVs, and established manufacturers expanded their electrified lineups during the same period ([El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/autopistas/el-efecto-de-tesla-en-el-mercado-mexicano/)).

The Mexico report documents changes to stores, charging, prices, financing, and online sales. It does not document a Mexico-first vehicle or a local software package comparable to the Chinese examples.

**Javier Verdura**, born in Mexico City, joined Tesla in 2012 and became global director of product design. His work included Supercharger hardware, Powerwall, and Solar Roof. He also maintained academic links with Mexico through the Universidad de Monterrey and design forums. Verdura held a global design role; he did not manage Tesla México’s sales operations ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2014/07/27/javier-verdura-director-of-product-design-and-project-management-for-tesla-motors-pulls-back-the-curtain/); [UDEM](https://www.udem.edu.mx/es/institucional/noticia/se-adelanta-udem-al-futuro-con-nuevos-espacios-y-experiencias)).

## 7. Prices, Sales, and Competitors

### China: Sales Growth and Falling Market Share

Estimated domestic Tesla sales in China rose as follows:

| Year | Estimated Tesla sales in China |
|---|---:|
| 2020 | 137,000 |
| 2021 | 321,000 |
| 2022 | 440,000 |
| 2023 | 604,000 |
| 2024 | 657,000 |
| 2025 | 626,000 |

The 2024 figure represented approximately **36.7% of Tesla’s global deliveries**. China became Tesla’s largest single country market in 2025, despite the decline in estimated sales that year ([Tridens](https://tridenstechnology.com/tesla-sales-statistics/); [Kaohoon/Bloomberg](https://www.kaohooninternational.com/markets/550400); [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/04/02/tesla-q1-2026-global-deliveries/)).

BYD, NIO, XPeng, Li Auto, Xiaomi, and other domestic brands released new models frequently and competed on prices, software, and interiors. BYD passed Tesla in quarterly global battery-electric sales in the fourth quarter of 2023. Tesla’s reported share of China’s new-energy-vehicle market fell from roughly **10% in 2022 to under 7% in 2025** ([The Revere](https://the-revere.com/2024/04/byd-surpasses-tesla-in-global-ev-sales/); [ESG Times](https://www.esgtimes.in/mobility/ev/china-strengthens-grip-on-ev-market-as-byd-overtakes-tesla-in-sales/)).

### Mexico: Gutiérrez, Price Cuts, and Competing Brands

In January 2024, Tesla appointed **Teresa “Terry” Gutiérrez Smith** as country manager and CEO of Tesla México. A chemical engineer with an MBA from IPADE, she had worked at Procter & Gamble, McKinsey, Nestlé, Mattel, and Rappi. Her experience covered consumer brands, strategy, digital marketing, and online platforms ([Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/business/got-1-min-tesla-mexico-picks-ceo/); [Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/finance/news/rappi-appoints-new-ceo-former-ceo-moves-tesla); [Mexico SMT](https://mexicosmt.com/tesla-appoints-teresa-gutierrez-smith-as-mexico-country-manager-amidst-gigafactory-expansion/)).

Gutiérrez joined after nearly a decade of Tesla sales and charging operations in Mexico. Public sources do not assign each decision during her tenure to her personally.

The base Model 3 price moved from **MXN 881,900 to MXN 749,900 in August 2024**, and entry pricing reached approximately **MXN 626,500 in 2025**. The overall reduction was approximately **29.0%**. Tesla also offered financing and trade-in programs ([Motor Pasión](https://www.motorpasion.com.mx/industria/tesla-model-3-precio-mexico); [ADN40](https://www.adn40.mx/es-tendencia/2025-02-10/precio-tesla-model-3-en-mexico-2025)).

Approximately **41% of reported 2025 EV sales** in Mexico were below US$30,000. Tesla’s lowest-priced vehicle remained above this range ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/es/industry-reports/mexico-electric-car-market)).

BYD sold approximately **40,000 vehicles in Mexico in 2024 and 85,000 in 2025**, according to estimates cited in the Mexico report. Chinese brands collectively accounted for approximately **15% of Mexico’s new-vehicle market in 2025**, around 244,000 units. BYD sells different vehicle types at lower prices, so these totals do not provide a direct comparison with Tesla. They do show sales volumes well above Tesla’s estimated low-thousands annual sales ([El Economista via ICCT](https://theicct.org/publication/evolucion-del-mercado-de-vehiculos-electricos-ligeros-en-mexico-al-cierre-de-2024-jul25/); [El Imparcial](https://www.elimparcial.com/mexico/2026/01/14/mientras-autos-chinos-como-byd-ya-ganaron-mas-terreno-en-mexico-los-aranceles-del-50-de-claudia-sheinbaum-comenzaron-a-afectar-los-precios-el-empleo-y-el-comercio-en-este-y-otros-sectores-desde-el-1-de-enero/)).

Conventional hybrids represented approximately **79% of Mexico’s “eco” vehicle sales in early 2025**. These vehicles do not need public charging and compete for some of the same customers considering lower fuel costs ([Remedia/AMIA](https://remedia.bio/index.php/2025/04/08/sube-26-venta-de-coches-electricos-e-hibridos-en-mexico/)).

The “eco” share of new light-vehicle sales increased from **0.5% in 2016 to 9.7% in 2025 and 12.8% through August 2026**. This category includes hybrids, so it does not measure battery-electric or Tesla sales ([TResearch/INEGI](https://www.tresearch.mx/post/venta-de-autos-electricos)).

By 2026, conference materials described Gutiérrez as Tesla México’s former country manager. The public sources do not explain her departure or link it to sales results ([AVAL Tech Week](https://avaltechweek.com/speaker?id)).

## 8. Factories

Local production changed Tesla’s costs and prices in China. In Mexico, the announced plant did not reach construction, and Tesla continued to sell imported vehicles.

### China: Shanghai Production

Tesla signed the Shanghai factory agreement in July 2018 after China removed foreign-ownership limits for electric-vehicle producers. Construction began on January 7, 2019, and the first China-made Model 3 cars were delivered on December 30, **357 days later** ([Xinhua](http://www.xinhuanet.com/english/2019-01/08/c_137726817.htm); [China Daily](https://subsites.chinadaily.com.cn/lingang/2019-12/19/c_463228_7.htm)).

Local production reduced Tesla’s exposure to import tariffs and made its vehicles eligible for purchase benefits that had favored locally made cars. Tesla also bought batteries from CATL and increased local sourcing. Local content passed 90% in 2021, exceeded 95% during 2023–2025, and reached a reported 98% in 2026 across more than 400 tier-one suppliers ([East-West Center](https://www.eastwestcenter.org/sites/default/files/private/apb152harwit.pdf); [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/11/06/tesla-shanghai-plant-localization-98/)).

These changes supported the Model 3 price reductions described in Section 6. The reported **65% saving** for Shanghai refers to capital spending per unit of factory capacity compared with the U.S. Model 3 line. It does not mean that every vehicle cost 65% less to produce ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3640303694901763)).

### Mexico: The Santa Catarina Announcement

Tesla announced the Santa Catarina project in February 2023. The initial investment figure was **US$5 billion**. Later public figures reached approximately US$10 billion and then US$15 billion, with the largest number including expected supplier investment. These were announced amounts, not records of money already spent ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/2023/03/01/fabrica-da-tesla-no-mexico-exigira-investimentos-de-us-5-bilhoes-oficial/); [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/business/how-much-is-tesla-going-to-invest-in-nuevo-leon/)).

Musk slowed the project in October 2023, citing high interest rates and their effect on vehicle affordability. He announced a formal pause in July 2024, pointing to the risk of U.S. tariffs on Mexican-made vehicles. Nuevo León began road and water infrastructure for the site, but Tesla had not broken ground through 2026 ([Univision](https://www.univision.com/noticias/america-latina/tesla-fabrica-llega-dudas-monterrey-mexico-sabemos); [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2024/07/24/tesla-officially-pauses-giga-mexico-project-over-potential-trump-win/); [El CEO](https://elceo.com/negocios/el-paso-de-elon-musk-en-mexico-tesla-se-frena-pero-starlink-avanza/)).

| Topic | China | Mexico |
|---|---|---|
| Production status | Shanghai deliveries began in December 2019 | No Tesla vehicle production through 2026 |
| Construction | 357 days from groundbreaking to first deliveries | No groundbreaking |
| Vehicle sourcing | Local production alongside other Tesla plants | All vehicles imported |
| Supplier development | More than 400 tier-one suppliers; reported 98% local content in 2026 | Supplier announcements without an operating Tesla plant |
| Commercial effect | Lower tariff exposure, purchase benefits, local batteries, and lower prices | Prices remained tied to imports, exchange rates, transport, and financing |

## 9. Charging Access for Other Brands

China’s Tesla charging network reportedly exceeded **2,500 stations and 12,000 stalls by 2026**, with more than 950 stations open to non-Tesla GB/T vehicles. In February 2026, Tesla opened **19 Mexican sites and 88 stalls** to selected vehicles from other brands, starting with Audi and Porsche through NACS ([Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Tesla%20Supercharger%20Network/7737406); [Motor Pasión](https://www.motorpasion.com.mx/industria/muchas-marcas-autos-compatibles-supercargadores-tesla-mexico-problema-que-nadie-compra-porque-ninguna-china)).

The two markets had different standards histories. Tesla added adapters and ports for China’s established **GB/T** standard. In Mexico, other manufacturers arranged access to Tesla’s **NACS** chargers. Open access could earn charging revenue and increase station use. It also gave owners of competing vehicles access to Tesla’s network.

## 11. Data Gaps

1. **Store counts:** The China report describes early store expansion but gives no consistent annual count. Additional company or registration data are needed for a numerical store comparison.
2. **Store performance:** Public sources do not provide sales per store, customer leads, the share of visitors who buy, service coverage, or city-level demand. These measures would help assess whether either country needed more stores.
3. **Sales:** Chinese figures come from a mix of company disclosures, registration data, and analyst estimates. Mexican Tesla sales are estimates because Tesla does not report consistently to INEGI/AMIA.
4. **Internal goals:** Tesla’s Mexican sales targets, margins, cost of winning each customer, and store budgets are unavailable. Public evidence cannot show whether Tesla met its own goals.
5. **Competitors:** BYD’s Mexican sales cover different models and price ranges. A model-by-model comparison is needed before treating its totals as a direct Tesla benchmark.
6. **Market categories:** Mexico’s “eco” category includes conventional hybrids. China’s new-energy-vehicle category also needs a consistent definition before comparing shares across countries.
7. **Charging:** Station counts are not adjusted for population, EV fleet, geography, stalls per station, or charger use. They show network size, not efficiency.
8. **Leadership:** Zhu’s appointment matches a period of major changes in Tesla China, but sales also depended on prices, products, policy, and market growth. Public sources do not show which Mexican decisions Gutiérrez personally directed.
